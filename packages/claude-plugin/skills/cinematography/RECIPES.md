# SC2 cutscene recipes

The trigger-level shape of each tier, as Blizzard's campaign map scripts and libraries build it, plus what was verified in game (5.0.17). Natives are checked against Blizzard's `natives.galaxy` plus `natives_missing.galaxy` from the public [sc2-game-data](https://github.com/sc2-arcade-watcher/sc2-game-data) mirror, whose maintainer added the natives the shipped file leaves out (the cutscene fades and filters among them). `natives.galaxy` alone is out of date: it hasn't changed since 5.0.0.

## Ping

A transmission, plus `CameraPan` (or `CameraApplyInfo` to a camera Anchor) over a couple of seconds with velocity −1 and decelerate 10. No Cinematic Mode. Then hand the camera back with `CameraRestore` if the pan moved the player off their work.

## Trigger scene

Blizzard splits each scene into four triggers run in turn with wait: **Setup → Cinematic → CinematicEnd → Cleanup**.

1. **Setup**, under black: `CinematicFade(false, ...)` out, `libNtve_gf_CinematicMode(true, players, 0)`, then `UISetMode(players, c_uiModeFullscreen, 0)` if the scene wants no letterbox bars. Cinematic Mode letterboxes by default. Then create the staged cast, hide or pause the live units, pause AI and time of day, lock camera input, and `CameraSave` mid-mission.
2. **Cinematic:** cut to the first camera object at 0 s, then `TriggerSkippableBegin(PlayerGroupAll(), 0, null, true, false)` and set `cinematicCompleted = false`. Fade in. Play the shots: `CameraApplyInfo(player, CameraInfoFromId(<anchor>), duration, -1, 10, true)` moves between camera objects, held under `TransmissionSendForPlayerSelect` lines with `libNtve_gf_CinematicPortrait(...)` (wait on each line, or send without waiting and use `TransmissionWait(TransmissionLastSent(), 0)`; both are clean back to back). The last action sets `cinematicCompleted = true`.
3. **CinematicEnd:** fade out, over 0.5 s if the flag is false (skipped) or 2.0 s if true.
4. **Cleanup**, under black: put the world in its one final state (remove the staged cast, restore units, doors and bounds, set the Light and fog back, unlock and unpause everything). Apply the gameplay camera (the start, or `CameraRestore`). Then hand back: fade in, `libNtve_gf_CinematicMode(false, players, d)` and the camera's settle to the gameplay view, all with one `d` = 1.5 s, or 1.0 s after a skip. Then select the player's units and issue or flash the objective.

A Mission's own trigger scene is a shot list on its camera Anchors in `missions/<id>/trigger-scenes/<id>.json`, which `compile_map` writes as these four triggers, on its scene's cue in place of the scene's Transmissions (sc2-adjutant's README, "trigger scene"; ADR 0012). It writes the cuts and eased moves (a shot's camera may be aimed at a unit with its Anchor's values: a unit Anchor's, which may be a Wave's unit, such as a raid's leader, filmed only once its send has created her, or `"cue"`, the unit the scene's cue keeps: the one an `anchorNears` cue came near, or the one an `effectStarts` cue's effect struck (its caster for an effect on a point), as `unit` or a move's `toUnit`; prefer `effectStarts` when the moment is a data effect landing on a unit, a strike or a cast, since it frames exactly what the data struck, as the named effect runs (name the first effect the search runs on the unit it found to open as the strike starts, not its damage, which comes as it hits), where `anchorNears` by type keeps the first unit of the type in a circle, not the one the data's search picked (CHECKS.md O5); Apply Camera Object without its target, then Pan Camera to the unit), the lines as Transmissions, the skip, both fades, AI time, the camera lock and save, and the handback on `handback.camera` or `CameraRestore`. Straight after a Cinematic on the same cue it shares that Cinematic's skip, and the Cinematic leaves the screen black, Cinematic Mode on and the music paused for its handback. It stages the scene's `cast` in Setup and removes it in Cleanup, hides or pauses the `live` units it names and puts them back, moves live units to `marks` under black, and walks staged or live units, or a player's units in a Zone (`player` and `inZone`, such as a garrison left behind), to a point at a shot's cut or `atMs` into it, removing those that `withdraw` on arrival (a Zone's units already there at once, and in Cleanup, on a skip those in the Zone at the order's moment). It warps units in round a point (`warpIns`), has a builder build a structure finished at once (`builds`), warps units or a player's army out (`warpOuts`; never the staged cast, which leaves by a `withdraw` or the Cleanup; `except` keeps named units, such as heroes who stay while the army is recalled), sends actor messages to a live unit or the staged cast (`actorEvents`: a model swap, a signal, a flash; to the cast, effects only, since Cleanup removes it), has a speaker speak from a staged unit (`speakers`: a voice on a holo-projector, its flash on the prop), and changes a live unit's owner and behaviours (`unitChanges`: a dormant device given to the player, its invulnerability removed and a power field added). Cleanup lands what a skip cut short: walked live units put at their last place (several sent to one place round it, not on one another), withdrawing ones removed, warp-ins created, builds finished, warped-out units removed (never those in `except`), and the lasting actor messages of each `actorEvents` entry not yet sent (a model swap, a signal) sent to its unit; its `effects` (a flash) are never replayed, and the owner and behaviours of each `unitChanges` entry not yet given set on its unit, if still alive, all in the scene's order with those messages and the warps (a unit given away before its old player's army warps out stays). A timed action gives nothing once the Cinematic has ended, so none starts in the fade out after a skip. It writes the scene's `music` too ("Music" below). Its `light`, `fog` and `mask: "off"` are switched under black in Setup and put back in Cleanup before the handback.

It starts the moment its cue fires, never waiting for an in-play line (ADR 0013): Setup cuts off only the in-play script line playing, and that line plays again from its start once the scene has handed back, followed by the rest of its cue. The Cleanup clears only the scene's own line (`TransmissionClear`), never `TransmissionClearAll()`, which would drop the player's other transmissions too (T9).

Its `handback` also writes the variants: lines after the handback (`linesFrom`, played in play as waited Transmissions once Cinematic Mode is off, T6, while the scene still holds the lines, so no in-play line cuts in), a handback with no fade that leaves the camera where it is (`fade: false`, an intended H3 exception), minimap pings on units (`ping`) and the objective shown straight after (`objective`, H3). Clocks are held for a run of scenes on one cue, the goal timers and the timers a Mechanic declares, until the last hands back (H4), and trigger scenes back to back on a cue share one skip as a Cinematic and the trigger scene after it do.

**Opening with no fade.** A short scene on a moment in play that the player must keep watching, such as a blink-strike's landing marker in a fight, opens in the player's sight rather than from black: `open: { "fade": false }`. Setup goes to no black. Cinematic Mode comes on over the ease, and the camera is locked and saved as for any scene. The first shot's camera is reached by an eased pan from the player's camera, `CameraApplyInfo` over `easeMs` (1000 when left out) with velocity −1 and decelerate 10, in place of the cut and the fade in. The skip begins with the ease, and a skip during it goes to the cleanup as any skip does. The shot's move and its lines start once the ease ends, and what it gives at its cut (orders, actor events, unit changes, warps) happens in sight, as at any later cut. Pair it with `handback.fade: false` for a scene with no black at either end. Everything Setup would do under black would happen in sight, so a scene that opens with no fade has no `cast`, `marks`, hidden `live` units (pausing one is fine), `light`, `fog` or `mask`; and a scene played straight after a Cinematic or another trigger scene on its cue opens on that scene's black, so it opens from black. The first scene of a chain may open with no fade. Use it only where the black would hide what the scene is for. A scene that teaches the map, or stages anything, opens from black.

A scene meant to follow another by a beat ("30 s after Message 4") is cued on that scene having played (`scenePlayed`, with `afterSeconds`), not on the other's cue plus a guessed length: it fires once the other's last line has ended or its trigger scene has handed back (a chain's, once its last scene has), so the gap holds however long the other queued behind lines already playing. A part that answers an earlier one but plays on a cue of its own ("I was correct" after the warning) takes `after` on its entry, naming the earlier part: it still plays on its own cue, but if that comes first it waits for the earlier part and plays behind it, so the parts can't play out of order. If the earlier part no longer makes sense once the later one is due (a "we may have to pull out" when the withdrawal has begun), give the earlier part's entry `skipIfLate: true` as well: it's dropped when the later part's cue comes first, and the later part plays at once rather than behind it. Whether a message takes it is the author's call.

A Beat that is a scene (a call whose warning is the Beat, with the objective it gives) starts on that scene playing (the Beat's `startsWhen: [{ "type": "scenePlays", "scene": 8 }]`), not on the scene's cue copied onto the Beat: it starts as the scene does, after its cue entry's `afterSeconds`, so its reveals and objective come with the call, and the scenes cued on its start queue behind the call's lines. A Beat started by a Cinematic or trigger scene starts as it plays, and what it starts (its reveals, its Mechanics' actions, the scenes cued on its start) waits for the handback, so nothing plays over the scene or runs a clock under it (H4).

A scene of one-off lines, each for its own moment in play (one on the first structure lost, one on the second), cues each line on its own (a cue entry's `line`, its sound ID), not the whole scene on the first moment, which plays them all at once. A scene of several messages, each after a stage direction, cues each part with `from` instead. A sound ID may count the line's place in its scene, so after an edit at the Source pull with `refresh-screenplay`, which moves the cue with its line; a plain pull refuses a cued line that moved.

**Victory outro.** On the victory condition, a trigger scene ends the game instead of handing back, as Blizzard's victory cinematics do: Cinematic Mode, a held camera object (about 4 s), the fade out, then the victory. A skip goes straight to the victory through the 0.5 s fade. Its cleanup puts nothing back for play: the screen stays black, Cinematic Mode on, the camera locked and the units paused, and `GameOver(1, c_gameOverVictory, true, true)` follows. A Mission's trigger scene declares `"outro": "victory"` and no `handback`, and plays last on a victory outcome's cue, with no delay. The cue's trigger runs the Mechanics' victory actions first, then the outro, then the victory, with no death pause after it (the outro is that pause). T4's budget is the outro's: about 25 s, warn over 45. A closing shot with no lines needs no Screenplay scene: list it by `"outcome": "victory"` in place of a `scene`, with no cue, and it plays last on every victory condition, its shots giving no `line`; a scene cued last on that condition hands on to it, sharing its skip, and the first victory condition met plays it once.

Gotchas:
- Turning Cinematic Mode **off** in the default Mission transition style clears every transmission, which cuts off a line still playing. Wait for the last line first.
- **Don't call Story Mode.** Its native is restricted to Blizzard's maps. `libNtve_gf_StoryMode` hides the UI and sets the screen mode itself, then calls the native, which in a custom map logs `Trigger Error in '<trigger>': Calling StoryMode is not authorized` on screen. `CampaignMode` is restricted the same way. Use Cinematic Mode, then `UISetMode(players, c_uiModeFullscreen, 0)` (Set Screen Mode) to drop the bars.
- `FogSetDensity`, `FogSetColor`, `FogSetStartHeight`, `FogSetFallOff` and their `...OverTime` forms work, subtly from the gameplay camera. Set the fog back in cleanup.

## Cinematic

Blizzard's NCO and LotV `.SC2Cutscene` files, and their per-cinematic Lights, load through a dependency on the story mods (NovaStoryAssets, VoidStory). There are two ways to play one, and both were verified:

- **In a full-screen dialog** (LotV's `libVoiC_gf_CampaignPlayCutscene2`): Cinematic Mode on, then Set Screen Mode to Fullscreen (LotV turns Story Mode on here, which our maps may not call; see the gotcha above); `DialogCreate` → `DialogSetFullscreen` → `DialogSetImageVisible(false)` → `DialogSetVisible`; `DialogControlCreate(dialog, c_triggerControlTypeCutscene)` → `DialogControlSetFullDialog` → `libNtve_gf_SetDialogItemCutscene(control, "Cutscenes\\<file>.SC2Cutscene", players)` → `libNtve_gf_SetDialogItemRenderType(control, c_triggerRenderTypeHDR, players)`; `CutscenePlay(CutsceneGetTriggerControl(control))`. This draws above the game world, so it works over a faded-out map.
- **In the world:** `CutsceneCreateNoPosition("Cutscenes\\<file>.SC2Cutscene", players, true)`, or `CutsceneCreateNew(file, point, facing, players, true)` to place it.

In both, wrap the wait in `TriggerSkippableBegin` with `WaitForCutsceneToEnd`, and have the skip path call `CutsceneStop`. Use the file's own fades or `CutsceneFade` for black.

A Mission's own cinematic is a Cinematic: a shot list in `missions/<id>/cinematics/<id>.json` on imported Sets, which `compile_map` writes with the fades, the ConversationDriver over the scene's lines, each speaker's lines on its cast actor with its model's Talk over its Stand (a shot's timed `anims` replace the Stand under it, and a plain anim name replaces both), camera moves (`to`), per-shot light rigs (`lights`) and base-Light switches on a cut (`light`), several sets at their offsets, and the End Scene marker (sc2-adjutant's README, "Cinematic"). Its scene's cue plays it this way, in place of the scene's Transmissions, with the one cleanup below and then the scenes cued after it. The server's `compile_cutscene` tool with `play` loops one from map start in the full-screen dialog, for a check in game, when its scene isn't cued at mission start. A Campaign Mod's hub chat or interlude scene is a Cinematic the same way, in `mods/<id>/cinematics/` on Sets in `mods/<id>/sets/`, which `compile_mod` writes as the cutscene its cue plays; its lines are named by the Sounds the Conversation plays (docs/reference/mod-cinematics.md).

Gotchas:
- **Hide the map behind a dialog cinematic.** The dialog draws above the world, but a set's windows show the map rendered behind it. Before `CutscenePlay`, run Show/Hide Environment (`EnvironmentShow(c_environmentAll, false)`), and show it again in the one cleanup: it's global state (`docs/reference/editor-and-engine.md`, "Hide the map behind a Cinematic"). Units aren't environment and show through too, so pause and hide them: each unit on the map that isn't paused (or hidden) already goes into a group and is paused (or hidden), and the cleanup puts back just those groups, leaving the units the mission hid hidden. A Cinematic filmed on the map's own ground (a Kept cutscene's set, placed back in map coordinates by its `offset`) keeps the terrain: its Blueprint entry's `"environment": "shown"` leaves the environment out of both and plays it in the world at Point(64, 64), where those coordinates meet the terrain, not in a dialog; it still hides the units.
- A world-space cutscene played under `CinematicFade` to black is invisible, because that fade blacks out the game world. Use the dialog, or leave the world unfaded.
- After one in-world run under `CinematicFade` the map stayed in the cutscene's pale Light. The cause isn't confirmed: runs in a dialog, and in the world without `CinematicFade`, didn't leave it. So set the mission Light back with `GameSetLighting(<mission light>, 0)` after every cinematic. Whether the dialog path needs it too isn't known yet; a Cinematic's cleanup sets it anyway.
- A long Blizzard file (such as `SMX3_Cutscene_MP02_020`, about 6 minutes) is played in sections with `CutsceneGoToBookmark`. Played from the start it shows only its opening. Lines inside the file are driven by the owning mission's conversation data, so they may be silent elsewhere.
- Depth of field: use the HotS-style blur amount (`doftype 0`, `depthOfField` 0.1-0.5, `focalDepth`, falloffs; `blur` on a ShotCamera), even though LotV's files use an f-stop: physical f-stop (`doftype 2`) doesn't render in the Mac client. Judge it only in game, since the Director Window draws depth of field badly.
- A cutscene over a map's own set plays in the world at the origin its terrain was imported at, `CutsceneCreateNew(file, Point(64, 64), 0.0, players, true)`; the server's `compile_cutscene` tool with `play` writes it into the Mission's Map Package and plays it in a loop from map start. Each run pauses AI time and restores it at the end. Escape is meant to skip a run: the run waits in its own skippable trigger, run with wait, so a skip ends only that trigger and the loop's one `CutsceneStop` and AI restore still run. This isn't tested in game yet.
- Cinematic Mode (`CinematicMode`, `libNtve_gf_CinematicMode`) doesn't pause AI time, so timed waves keep coming under a cutscene. Pause it yourself ("Pause AI Time", `AITimePause(true)`) and restore it in the one cleanup, skip included, unless it was already paused ("AI Time Is Paused", `AITimeIsPaused()`).
- A walker's animation carries its model's own length and move speed, recorded with `record_model_anims` (the Host Adapter's `model.export`) into the Workspace's `model-anims.json`, or already in core's. Without them it stands still on its path, and values copied from another model are a guess.

The evidence for these is in sc2-adjutant's `docs/reference/editor-and-engine.md`.

## Handheld camera

A hand-built cutscene's trick: the camera rides an invisible marine's head while the marine plays a slowed anim, so the frame drifts as a held camera does. In a Cinematic it's a cast member, `MarineXPR` at `opacity` 0 looping `Stand` at `animSpeed` 0.2-0.5 (a `Walk` bobs, a `Flail` shakes hard), and a shot's `cameraAttach` on its `Ref_Head`, offset z −0.3 to −0.6, with the shot camera's target at the marine (BUILT-SETS.md, "Riders and riding cameras"). Blizzard never attaches a camera; their handheld wobble is keyed by hand, a shot's `shake`. Not checked in game as a Cinematic yet.

## Music

How Blizzard plays music, from its mission scripts, campaign libraries and cutscene files. Nothing here is tested in game yet. A Cinematic's cues are its `sounds`, which the compiler writes as Sound nodes; a trigger scene's cue sheet is built from these by hand.

- **Pause the mission music** at the scene's start, under black: `SoundtrackPause(PlayerGroupAll(), c_soundtrackCategoryMusic, true, true)` (pause, with fade), and the same for `c_soundtrackCategoryAmbience` if the scene brings its own ambience. That's all the campaign libraries do around a cinematic (`libVoiC_gf_PlayCinematic`). Cleanup unpauses both with a fade (`..., false, true)`). Alternatively, stop it with `SoundtrackStop(..., c_soundtrackCategoryMusic, true)` and play the mission's main id again with `makeDefault = true` at the handback, as LotV missions do. A Cinematic's trigger and its `--play` loop pause and unpause both this way, since a Cinematic brings its own sets' ambience, and a playtest heard the mission's ambience play under one before they did.
- **A cue from the top:** `SoundtrackPlay(PlayerGroupAll(), c_soundtrackCategoryMusic, "<id>", c_soundtrackCueAny, c_soundtrackIndexAny, false)`, with a one-shot single-file id. `SoundtrackWait("<id>")` waits for it to end. `SoundtrackStop(..., true)` fades it out.
- **A cue from an in-point:** `SoundPlayForPlayer(SoundLink("<Sound id>", -1), c_maxPlayers, PlayerGroupAll(), 100.0, <offset>)` (core's `playSound` primitive), then keep the handle with `SoundLastPlayed()` to `SoundStop(handle, true)` it later. This is the shape of Blizzard's `libHots_gf_PlaySoundLinkasMusic`, which pauses the Music soundtrack, plays a Music Sound, waits, then unpauses (HotS's `SwarmLib.galaxy`). **To verify in game:** that `offset` is in seconds, and that the Sound plays on the Music channel at music volume.
- **Crossfade:** stop the outgoing cue with fade and start the next in the same trigger step. The fade length is the engine's, not ours; for a longer crossfade, ramp the outgoing sound down with `SoundSetVolume` over a few waits first. **To verify:** the engine's fade length.
- **Stingers** are Sounds played over the bed, with `SoundPlayForPlayer` and offset 0.
- **Under lines:** `libNtve_gf_SetAllSoundChannelVolumes(libNtve_ge_VolumeChannelMode_Speech, PlayerGroupAll(), 0.5)` before the first line, and `..._Game` at the handback. Speech mode puts Music at 70% (NativeLib's helper; WoL missions switch to it before each transmission).
- **Silence:** `SoundtrackPlay(..., "Music_Silence", ..., true)` holds silence as the default, so nothing else fills it.
- **In a cinematic (`.SC2Cutscene`),** music can sit on the cutscene's own timeline as a Sound node whose `soundLink` is a Music Sound (36 in Blizzard's 336 cutscene files). A Cinematic's `sounds` write these (BUILT-SETS.md, "Sounds, look-ats and attached lamps"), its in-point as the node's `startOffset`, as Blizzard's music nodes give one. **To verify in game:** that the in-point plays from there, and that a Sound node with no `assetDuration` (the compiler doesn't know a file's length) plays and loops as the Editor's do.
- **Cleanup** (skip and natural end): stop every scene cue with fade (`SoundtrackStop`, `SoundStop` on each kept handle), set Game mode, and unpause or restart the mission music (M4).
- **A Mission's trigger scene** declares these as its `music` and `compile_map` writes them (sc2-adjutant's README, "trigger scene"). `mission: "pause"`, the default, pauses the mission music with a fade in Setup; `"keep"` leaves it playing in Speech mode, for a scene that should sit over the mission's own music. Each of `cues` is a Music Sound from an in-point (`fromMs`), on a `line` or `atMs`, faded from `untilMs` over `fadeOutMs` (`SoundSetVolume` steps, then `SoundStop`), in a trigger of its own so the lines keep their time; over kept music it pauses the soundtrack with a fade first. The cleanup stops each with a fade, sets Game mode and unpauses the music.

## Backdrops per shot

Dress the map for the space the action happens in (the street, the set, its light) and stop at a believable middle distance. What only one frame sees, the sky and a far skyline, is composited per shot in the cutscene, not dressed map-wide. A map-wide backdrop becomes a loop of tweaks: a city skybox covers only part of the dome and glows, and each tower added to fill it either vanishes into the glow or takes over the frame.

Place the sky by the round trip, not by computing it: the author places the sky actors in the Cutscene Editor through the shot's camera and saves, and the generator takes the actor transforms back (the file position plus the origin gives map coordinates). Expect rough placements, since the Editor's mouse controls on the Mac are imprecise. Some skies are flat cards that need a full x/y/z rotation to stand up behind the set: `KorhalSkyboxCityNight` took about (98.7, 12.4, -76.4), and two copies side by side to fill a wide frame. In a Cinematic, a per-shot backdrop is a Set piece tied to the camera it was placed for; [`BUILT-SETS.md`](BUILT-SETS.md) has the round trip for a set built by hand.

## Test it

Build an `exp-*` copy of an Editor-saved reference map with the scene's triggers, and have the author run Test Document and report back with screenshots. Each test map is a copy of the reference map whose triggers are replaced by one custom script (a `CustomScript` element in `Triggers`, mirrored in `MapScript.galaxy`) that labels each step on screen as it runs. Ask one question per map, and judge the result from screenshots.

Judge every cutscene in game, never from the Cutscene Editor alone: its preview misrepresents depth of field and doesn't show the map, so it can make a good shot look broken or hide a real problem. Every cutscene handed to the author comes with an in-game test. The `compile_cutscene` tool with `play` gives a test map that plays the cutscene from map start, with the game UI, the cursor and Test Document's error output hidden, for a clean frame (check the Editor's own error list for real script errors).

Test Document runs the map's `MapScript.galaxy` as it stands; only an Editor save rebuilds it from Triggers. A tool adding Galaxy writes it into both, as core's compiler does.
