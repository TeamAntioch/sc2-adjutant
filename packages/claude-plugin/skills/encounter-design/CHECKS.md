# Encounter checks

Apply every check to the encounter record and, once built, to the map. Record each as passed, fixed, an issue, or intended (with the reason). The principles are in `SKILL.md`; their backing is in [`PRINCIPLES.md`](PRINCIPLES.md).

## Problem and tool (T)

- **T1 One line.** The intended problem fits in one sentence and names what makes it hard. (EN-1)
- **T2 Built for a tool,** and the tool is needed on a first playthrough on Normal: name what happens without it. (EN-10)
- **T3 Brute force is costly, not impossible.** The heroes alone can fight through, at a real cost; the encounter is still winnable with only the guaranteed heroes. (EN-10)
- **T4 Teaching order.** A tool or role the encounter tests was met alone earlier in the mission or the campaign. (EN-1; `hero-mission-design` HM-3)
- **T5 Combos last long enough.** Every stun, root, wind-up or channel the encounter relies on for a two-hero follow-up lasts long enough to switch heroes and act. (EN-9)

## Space (S)

- **S1 Readable first.** The player can see the encounter from its observation spot before triggering it; any ambush is spaced to be survivable. (EN-2)
- **S2 Positioning tool.** At least one: high ground, choke or ramp, sight blocker, kiting loop. (EN-3)
- **S3 Second exit** from any narrow space. (EN-3)
- **S4 One screen.** The setup fits one screen at the campaign's default zoom, two at most. (EN-4)
- **S5 Sized from the kit.** Distances (cover to exit, choke widths, ramp widths) come from the heroes' ranges and speeds and the campaign's building codes. (EN-4)
- **S6 Chokes on the slower hero's route,** where the encounter has a choke the more mobile hero could skip. (EN-10)
- **S7 North-tall, south-low.** Nothing tall stands south of the action; no unit or prop the player must see hides behind a cliff or doodad to its south. (EN-13)
- **S8 Dressing changes no footprint.** Pathing and sight-blocking are the same before and after decoration. (EN-13)

## Enemies (E)

- **E1 Roles, not bodies.** 2–3 roles, one mutually covering pair, a named problem enemy and key enemy. (EN-7)
- **E2 Visible counters.** The player can tell, from the units' look and behaviour, which tool answers which role. (EN-7)
- **E3 Two states.** An aggressive territory, a fallback, and a switch condition the player can cause. (EN-6)
- **E4 Local.** No patrol crosses into another encounter; the encounter can't be seen or heard from the next one; a distraction, where the kits have one, pulls only enemies in this encounter. (EN-5)

## Telegraphs and stealth (A)

- **A1 Visible spawns.** Every enemy that arrives comes from a source the player can see, fitting its type. (EN-8)
- **A2 Alarm.** Reinforcements are announced by the standard alarm before they arrive. (EN-8)
- **A3 Retry varies.** Scripted arrivals change between retries through spawn slots, never by chance inside an attempt. (EN-8)
- **A4 Detection is true.** Each detection visual matches its real area exactly; the rules are the same for every enemy of a type; patrols repeat exactly. (EN-12)
- **A5 Guards react, then reset.** Noise, bodies and alarms draw an investigation of fixed length, then the guard returns. (EN-12)
- **A6 Stealth is optional.** Being detected starts a fight or slows the moment; it never fails the mission by itself. (EN-12)

## Props (P)

- **P1 Each prop gives a decision** and carries the campaign's prop telegraph. (EN-14)
- **P2 Second way through.** The encounter is solvable with the prop disabled, used early or destroyed by a stray shot. (EN-14)
- **P3 Same everywhere.** The prop behaves as it does in every other mission, and no look-alike decorative doodad sits near it. (EN-14)
- **P4 Its area is shown.** A prop that hurts or blocks shows its exact area (blast radius, fall line, cone). (EN-14)
