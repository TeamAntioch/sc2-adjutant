Use for Stage 2 geography sketching, when laying out a new mission's map, when reviewing or changing zones and connections, or when asked whether a layout will play well.
---

Lay out or review a Mission's geography: its Zone graph, the shapes and elevations of its Zones, and how play moves through them across Beats. This is Co-design Stage 2, the geography sketch. For a hero mission with no base (an installation mission), plan its structure with the `hero-mission-design` skill and its encounters with `encounter-design` first; their principles say which of this skill's rules change. Read and edit the Blueprint with the sc2-adjutant MCP server's tools (`get_blueprint`, `patch_blueprint`, `set_zone_polygon`, `move_zone`, `move_anchor`). Its fields are core's Mission Blueprint schema and ADR 0003 (sc2-adjutant's `docs/adr/0003-layout-model-for-the-level-design-checker.md`).

The rules are this skill's **Layout Principles** (LP-1 to LP-17, below) and its **pacing checks** (PC-1 to PC-6, in [`JUDGEMENT-CHECKS.md`](JUDGEMENT-CHECKS.md)). Cite them by id. [`PRINCIPLES.md`](PRINCIPLES.md) gives each one's backing: the Blizzard missions that follow or bend it, with their measured numbers, and the public ideas it builds on.

When a finding needs its reasoning, read the principle's backing in [`PRINCIPLES.md`](PRINCIPLES.md). Where this guidance and the schema differ, the schema and ADR 0003 (in sc2-adjutant's `docs/adr/`) win.

**Measured numbers win over placeholders.** When [`PRINCIPLES.md`](PRINCIPLES.md), [`ARCHETYPES.md`](ARCHETYPES.md) or **Enemy bases** below gives a number measured on Blizzard's missions (enemy strength, base contents, distances, timings), size the feature from it, not from values already in the blueprint: those are often placeholders. Before compiling, compare the blueprint's values with those numbers and tell the author where they differ. A mission sized from a hand-written guard list, at a third of Blizzard's median enemy count, reads as one a small army can steamroll.

**Corrections go into the guidance.** When the author corrects a design, put the lesson into the skill or Style Pack guidance for that work (this skill, `exemplar-design`, or the active Style Pack's Design Lessons) in the same change, and tell them where it went.

## Leading words

- **Critical path**: the ground route from the start to each goal. A *goal* is where a primary objective or victory condition sends the player.
- **Refuge**: a sheltered spot to look out from: the base, a regroup pocket, or high ground. In the blueprint it's a Zone whose `role` is `refuge` or `base`, and every Zone a Mechanic treats as shelter (such as a hazard's `safeZoneIds`) gets the `refuge` role.
- **Prospect**: open, exposed ground that a refuge overlooks. Play moves from refuge to prospect to the next refuge.
- **Overlook**: Zone A sees Zone B. A higher Zone overlooks the lower Zones it borders; `overlooks` adds long views.
- **Introduce, develop, twist, test**: the order every new Mechanic, enemy type or gate goes through across Beats. `introduces` records the first step.
- **Enemies are walls**: in an RTS most obstacles are units. An enemy group across the direct line from entry to exit gates a Zone until its Beat.
- **Archetype**: the mission type that sets the graph's shape. See [`ARCHETYPES.md`](ARCHETYPES.md).

## Layout Principles

- **LP-1 Mechanic first.** The Mission's Mechanic shapes the ground: pick the Mechanic and archetype before drawing Zones. Every Mechanic has terrain to act on, and abilities the mission grants have terrain that rewards them.
- **LP-2 Graph, then polygons, then population.** Work program, then Zone graph, then polygons, then Placements, then dressing. Critique topology before population, and dress only once the topology has settled.
- **LP-3 Teach in order.** Introduce, develop, twist, test: one new thing per Beat, first met away from heavy pressure, and shown acting on units other than the player's before the player has to face it. The climax combines things already taught, and each gate comes after the Beat that teaches what passes it.
- **LP-4 Read before committing.** The player previews each goal before entering it: a scripted reveal, a camera pan or tour, an overlook or a watchtower. Blizzard mostly uses the script: all four Blizzard missions in [`PRINCIPLES.md`](PRINCIPLES.md) preview their goals that way. A blind uphill entry is a deliberate climax, and early or easy missions keep main threats in plain sight.
- **LP-5 Refuge, then prospect.** Play moves from refuge to prospect to the next refuge, and refuges overlook the prospects. Refuge spacing in travel time sets the pace.
- **LP-6 Pace through contrast.** Loud Zones are separated by quiet ones, with a refuge before each climax and a reward after it. SC2 pacing is partly temporal, so the quiet can be a gap between waves. The pacing checks spell it out.
- **LP-7 Enemies are walls.** Place strongpoints across the line between entry and exit, and choose wide or narrow ground for each big threat on purpose.
- **LP-8 Layered stronghold.** An assault's core sits behind at least two enemy-held Zones, and threat doesn't fall over the last two or three.
- **LP-9 Show, then withhold.** Glimpse the final goal early (an opening pan, an overlook, a first-Beat tour past the Zones later Beats use) and show it again closer as the Beats advance. A Zone that opens at a Beat is visible from a Zone opened earlier.
- **LP-10 Cue every branch, price every risk.** Each exit from a branch Zone has a cue visible from the branch, and each route to a goal is marked by repeated doodads or rising elevation: in a study of players choosing between paths, a line of repeated structures and rising ground steered most of them, while one taller building changed nothing. A scout can see what defends each optional reward and what it holds.
- **LP-11 Refuges stay safe** unless one Beat scripts a breach. Long Transmissions play while the player is in a safe Zone, and story-critical units start in one, as The Evacuation keeps its key scientist in a fortified spot out of harm's way.
- **LP-12 Rewards one step off the path.** Optional rewards sit one Connection off the critical path, cued and scoutable. Vaults are small single-entrance pockets, rich resources sit out where they're exposed, and true secrets are rare.
- **LP-13 Open the map gradually.** One or two new directions at a time, no more than about three goal sites in one Beat, and every goal reachable at the Beat that needs it. Blizzard reworked an early version of The Evacuation that sent the player to several sites at once: it was too stressful for an early mission.
- **LP-14 Real alternatives.** Outside the deliberately linear archetypes (Escort, Commando), each goal has two meaningfully different approaches, the graph is no long hallway, and a narrow-entry Zone has a second exit. Commando keeps one alternative: a loop near each high-threat Zone, so the squad can escape.
- **LP-15 Size for the army, measure in time.** The player's body is an army: escort roads stay wide, and a narrow Connection is a declared drama beat. Measure distance as travel time, not cells. Competitive SC2 maps are tuned in seconds of worker travel for the same reason.
- **LP-16 The ground tells the truth.** Declared Connections match the terrain, every elevation change reads from above, and dressing matches function: dangerous Zones don't look inviting and refuges don't look hostile.
- **LP-17 Every Zone has a job.** Large Zones carry a sub-feature unless they're arenas, and the mission's goals use most of the map rather than one cluster.

## Propose a layout

1. **Read Stage 1's output:** the Beats, objectives, Mechanics and screenplay in the blueprint. Done when you can name the Mechanic, every goal, and what each Beat asks of the player. If those don't exist yet, Stage 1 comes first.
2. **Pick an archetype** from [`ARCHETYPES.md`](ARCHETYPES.md) (sometimes two, one per act), and say why it fits the Mechanic and story. Declare it as the blueprint's `archetype`, and set `archetype` on the first Beat of any act that changes type.
3. **List the program:** every space the Beats need. That means the start and its refuge, each goal site, each threat, the refuges along the way, and optional vaults and bonus sites. Done when every objective, Mechanic and scene has a home.
4. **Draw two or three variants of the Zone graph** that differ in topology (where the loops are, which approach is overlooked, where the gate sits), not in decoration. Give each Zone a `role`, a `landform` and an elevation: a cliff level from 0 (a pit, 8 below ground) through 1 (ground) to 15, each level 2 above the one below. A ramp joins two Zones one level apart, so a storey of 4 is two levels with a terrace between. A cliff climbs one level at a time. Where backdrop rock meets ground two or more levels away, the compile terraces the rock between. Two floors (Zones that aren't backdrop) drawn against each other two levels apart can't be terraced: the game draws black squares there, and the compile warns. Put a Zone a level between them, or rock a few cells wide. Give each connection a `type`, plus `oneWay`, `opensAtBeat` and `closesAtBeat` where they apply. Fill in `introduces` on each Beat with Mechanic ids, enemy unit types (such as `Zealot`) and connection types (such as `collapsible`), and `reveals` with the Zones its script shows (a camera pan, a revealer, a ping, a tour). For each reveal, choose in advance the point the player should see: place a point Anchor there, in that Zone, and write `{ "zoneId": "<zone>", "at": "<anchor>", "radius": <cells> }`. A bare Zone id compiles to the widest circle inside the Zone, and the compile warns; `"region": true` explores the Zone's whole trigger region, which is rarely what you want. Add each timed attack to `waves`, with its spawn and target Zones, arrival, unit types, Beats and timing.
5. **Give the Zones rough polygons** on the map. Zones that connect must touch. Zones that touch at the same level are walkable whether or not a connection is declared, so declare it or put a cliff between them. Shape Zones to the setting rather than to a grid. Lay out enemy bases and guards by **Enemy bases** below.
   - **Bounds.** Keep walkable Zones inside the map's playable area. By default it is the box round the Zones that aren't backdrop, plus 3 cells, kept inside the Editor's inset (10 / 8 / 10 / 20 cells from the left, bottom, right and top). The camera looks past the bounds, most of all to the north, so fill the inset with ground the player is meant to see, such as a `backdrop` Zone, cliffs or the far side of a chasm. Don't end the play space there.
   - **Staging.** To stage the mission area by area, as Blizzard's campaigns do, give the Beat that opens an area a `playable`. That is a list of Zone ids, a Zone or Kept region id, a rectangle, or `"entire"` round a cutscene. Bound the camera with `camera.bounds` where the player should see only part of what they can reach, such as a stealth room.
   - **Transitions.** Where a Beat's start scene pans from the last area to the next, set `camera.transition: true`.
   - Bounds hold until a later Beat sets others. The `bounds` check says what falls outside them.
   - **Interiors and caves.** Where units walk below high ground they can't see, set the blueprint's `unexploredAreas` to `"black-reduced-radius"`. Under plain Black Mask the cliff tops above a unit show through the fog (sc2-adjutant's `docs/reference/editor-and-engine.md`, "Unexplored areas").
6. **Check each variant.** Run the server's `check_layout` and `preview` tools on the Mission. Fix each warning that contradicts what you intended. Record each intentional one in `missions/<id>/layout-notes.md` as the check id, the Zone and why. Then work through [`JUDGEMENT-CHECKS.md`](JUDGEMENT-CHECKS.md).
7. **Present the variants.** For each, give a short play narrative per Beat (what's open, what's visible, what the player does, how it should feel), the checker summary, and the trade-offs. Recommend one. Done when the author has chosen or approved a variant, its zones are in the blueprint, and `preview.svg` has been regenerated.

### Beside Kept ground

A Mission with Kept terrain (an imported map) draws Zones only where a job needs them. Each one sets `terrain`: `"generate"` for a new section whose ground the compile writes, `"kept"` for an area drawn over the map's own ground. Once one Zone sets it, every Zone must.

- A generating Zone's elevation is its cliff level. Where it meets Kept ground at another level it gets cliffs on its own edge, so give each crossing into Kept ground a Connection with an `at` on the seam, and match the Kept level there. `check_workspace` reports a Connection whose levels don't meet.
- Use `open` or `narrow` Connections across the seam. Ramps into Kept ground aren't generated yet: add one afterwards with the `add-ramp` brush (`edit_kept_terrain`).
- Kept doodads on a generating Zone's ground stay, moved with it, and its set pieces keep clear of them. To clear the ground, ask for that as its own brush edit first.
- `check_layout` skips Missions with Kept terrain, so walk the routes across the seam yourself.
- The package keeps its own playable area unless the Blueprint gives `bounds.playable`. Then the compile sets it in MapInfo, and puts the old one back if you drop the field. Give a rectangle here: `"zones"` would shrink the map to the box round the few Zones you drew, and `"editor"` to the Editor's inset. Beat bounds can name the map's Kept regions by their Kept References. Since `check_layout` doesn't run here, read the compile's warnings for what falls outside the bounds.

## Enemy bases

Enemy bases and their guards are `resource-base` and `encounter-group` Placements. They're built only when they have `at` (`ResourceBaseParamsSchema`, `EncounterGroupParamsSchema`). The numbers below are sized from Blizzard's, measured on the placed objects of 111 of its campaign maps.

- **A working base:** every enemy base is one, the main base and capturable camps included. That means a town hall (`townHall`) with about 8 workers (4 at a small site) and gas buildings on its geysers. A base without them doesn't read as a base.
- **Strength to match Blizzard:** about 120 enemy units on Normal across the map (Blizzard's median 119), sized from the numbers, never from a hand-written guard list.
  - camps: 8-9 guards each, lighter where a Beat teaches something;
  - main base: 25-45;
  - lanes: guard groups of about 6 on the ground between bases, 8-34 cells in front of each, off the ramps. Each step toward a base should be a fight before the base.
- **Shape:** minerals back onto a cliff or the map edge, away from the approach (`facing`). Defences face the approach (the group's `facing`) and stand on cliff edges overlooking the low ground, spread round the rim rather than plugging the ramp. A camp's Pylon and Cannons sit between its town hall and the approach, with its guards just in front.
- **Unit types:** the game's (sc2-adjutant core's `data/tech-catalog.json`) or the Workspace Campaign Mod's, which the server's `asset_catalog` tool lists, with which fly and which are structures. Use a Campaign Mod unit as a game one; the tech list and the air-bypass note read both.
- **Zone fit:** everything a base or camp holds stays inside its Zone. Captures and region triggers count by Zone, and the compiler warns about anything outside one.

## Critique a layout

1. **Read the intent:** the screenplay, Beats, objectives and Mechanics. List every mismatch between what the screenplay describes and what the blueprint builds (missing bases, enemies, shelters, destinations). These lead the report, because they outweigh any layout warning.
2. **Name the archetype** from the screenplay and Mechanic, not from the blueprint's current shape or the examples in [`ARCHETYPES.md`](ARCHETYPES.md). If the blueprint declares a different `archetype`, that's a mismatch to lead with; if it declares none, offer one under "Data to add".
3. **Run the checker and the preview:** the server's `check_layout` and `preview` tools on the Mission, then look at the preview image. Confirm that the start and goals the checker found are the ones the author means. To judge what only the game shows (sight, how far the player sees, how a Zone reads from the player's camera), call `capture_shots` with one Shot per Zone or chokepoint in a single call and read the frames; a Shot is a rectangle of cells (`rect: [left, bottom, right, top]`), about 10-20 cells for a doorway and 30-50 for a whole Zone, and the frames are saved as images you read. It needs the macOS Host Adapter and a compiled map; on another machine, ask the author to run it.
4. **Settle `terrain-agreement` first.** The route checks (`critical-path`, `travel-times`, `entrances`, `beat-gating`, `readable-approach`) trust the declared connections, so their results mean little until declared connections match the terrain.
5. **Sort every `warn`** into a real problem or an intended exception. Intended exceptions already recorded in `missions/<id>/layout-notes.md` count as settled. Where the intent isn't clear from the screenplay or Beats, the answer becomes an open question for the author.
6. **For every `not-evaluated`**, name the data that would let the check run, as an offer under "Data to add".
7. **Work through [`JUDGEMENT-CHECKS.md`](JUDGEMENT-CHECKS.md).**
8. **Report** under five headings:
   - Mismatches and open questions
   - Function (does it work)
   - Usability (can the player read it)
   - Delight (is it memorable)
   - Data to add

   Each finding gives what's wrong, the principle or check id, and a concrete blueprint edit, or the question that decides the edit. Phrase it in the leading words: "add a refuge with high ground past the ramp so the player overlooks the prospect", not "add cover". Done when every warning, every `not-evaluated` and every judgement check is accounted for.

## Reading the checker

- **Statuses:** `pass`, `warn` and `info`, or `not-evaluated` when inputs are missing. Each line starts with the check id (`terrain-agreement: …`), followed by the Layout Principles and pacing checks it comes from.
- **A full-map background Zone** listed first (a "surface") leaves every later Zone as an island that can be walked into from every side. Give open ground its own Zones between the refuges, and separate routes with cliffs or impassable ground.
- **Warnings are advisory.** The severities and bands are calibrated against one Blizzard mission per archetype (named in [`ARCHETYPES.md`](ARCHETYPES.md)), so they're starting points. When a warning conflicts with the archetype's intent, keep the design and record why.
- **What it derives:**
  - **Start:** player 1's first unit Anchor.
  - **Goals:** from primary objectives' `completeWhen` and from victory conditions. A `timer` goal means holding the start, so the route checks report nothing to route. A `resourceQuota` goal sends the player to every `resource-base` Placement's Zone, each from the first Beat that lists or reveals it.
  - **Enemy-held Zones:** `base` Zones, other than the start, that hold an encounter group or another player's unit.
  - **The player's held region:** the start plus the `base` and `refuge` Zones joined to it.
  - **Overlap:** later Zones win where polygons overlap, as in terrain generation.

  If any of these look wrong, fix the blueprint data rather than arguing with the report.
- **`threat-paths`** routes each ground wave from its spawn Zone's centre to each of its `targetZoneIds`, and reports where it enters the held region and when, at the wave's `speed` or a marine's. It lists the units each send brings, and apart from them those only the first send brings (`sends: "first"`, a leader who comes once). A leader who must be named, by a cue, a condition or a trigger scene, binds a unit Anchor with no position (the entry's `anchorId`, count at most 1 on each difficulty, in a ground Wave, ADR 0008); the checks place no unit there from the start, and her Wave is the threat. Give a repeating wave a unit that comes with every send on each difficulty its leader comes on, or the compile reports that its repeats bring nobody there. That arrival time is the defender's warning; compare it, and the interval, with the archetype's bands. A wave's `warning.seconds` is its Threat's telegraph. `safe-havens` lists the safe Zones waves attack, counting every candidate as attacked, and `teaching` asks about wave units no Beat introduces. Ground waves compile (ADR 0008) from a Beat that starts in the map (the first, or one with `startsWhen`); the compile reports the rest. Check each one against the screenplay and Mechanics.
- **Routes:** routes run on the cell grid, from the player's unit to the goal's edge (or its target Anchor). A goal for another unit to reach a Zone (an escorted convoy, a unit the player gets later) is routed from that unit, at its Anchor's `speed` when set. They cross between Zones only where a connection is declared (and, when the connection has an `at`, only within half its `width` of that point), and they go round enemy-held Zones where they can. Give ramps, bridges and chokes an `at` where the real crossing sits; without it a route may cross anywhere along the shared border, which makes travel times read short. A route that has to fight through says so.
- **Passive groups:** an `encounter-group` Placement with `passiveUntilBeat` doesn't attack until that Beat (a parade, a garrison caught unready). Before then `safe-havens` notes it instead of warning, and routes don't treat its base as a wall. It's checker-only: make sure the screenplay or a trigger actually holds the group back.
- **Single points of failure** warn only when the crossing can close or be destroyed (`collapsible`, `bridge`, `closesAtBeat`, or a Mechanic that closes it on a Schedule), and not in an escort or commando Beat, where one road is the design. Fixed ones are reported as usually intended.
- **`hazard-retreat`** is window versus crossing, for every Threat but plain combat. For a Threat on whole Zones (each `hazard` Mechanic, and any Mechanic like it that covers whole Zones) it measures the walk from the worst cell of every affected Zone to the nearest safe Zone, and asks for a warning of at least k× that, less a second of slack for sketched Zones. For a Threat on a route plus a radius (a patrol and its sight) it measures walking across the sight, against the gap between passes. k depends on what getting caught costs: 1.2 for damage, combat and failing the Mission, and 1 for a setback, which is a placeholder until a stealth mission is studied. A Mechanic whose countdown is always on screen warns for the whole countdown. `hazard` Mechanics aren't compiled; other Mechanics may be.
- **`checkpoint-spacing`** reports, for each setback Threat, how far the player is sent back when caught: the walk from the worst spot it reaches to its checkpoint. It warns only when a setback names no checkpoint; there's no calibrated band yet.
- **`closing-traps`** warns when a Connection that closes on a Schedule (`closesAtBeat`, a gate, a flooded ford) can shut while a Threat is active, and leaves part of the Threat's ground with no ground way to its safe ground. A Mechanic says the two happen together by giving its Threat and its Connection change the same Schedule.
- **`archetype-bands`** applies the declared archetype's calibrated bands to the Beats it covers: Hold's ground entrances, wave warning and interval; Assault's ways into each enemy base and goal travel times; and, for the others, how far the first goal is from the start. Structure and wave timing outside a band warn; travel times are reported. Escort and Moving hazard bands wait on later ADR 0004 data, and the check says so.
- **`bounds`** reads the map's playable area and each Beat's bounds (ADR-0015). It warns of five things:
  - a Zone that isn't backdrop running past the playable area inside the Editor's inset. The edge is an invisible wall across its ground. Ground in the inset itself is the camera's apron and isn't counted.
  - a Beat's Zones or Anchors outside the playable area it sets;
  - camera bounds reaching outside the playable area;
  - a camera Anchor, or a camera in a trigger scene cued as the Beat starts, aiming outside the camera bounds. Whether the game holds such a camera to the bounds isn't yet checked in game.
  - a unit or point more than 7.5 cells past the camera bounds.

  The compile adds advice of its own: a playable rectangle off the even cells, which the game rounds; a rectangle nearer the map's edge than the inset, where the void may show; and bounds on a Beat nothing starts.
- **`route-open`** (issue #749) reads the terrain the compile would generate, not the declared levels, and warns for each pair of Zones joined by a ground Connection (anything but `air-only`; `bridge` is left to the bridge's own terrain object) that touch on the map, where every cell pair along their shared border is blocked. A cell is blocked when it lies under a cliff mesh and isn't a ramp's lane or a terrain object's deck, or when the author's `pathingPaint` covers all of it. The finding names the Zones, the Connection types, their levels and up to four blocked cells. It reports only what the terrain data proves: one walkable cell pair anywhere along the border (so any ramp, however far from the Connection's `at`) passes it. Dug pools' and doodads' paint aren't read (the compile warns about doodad cuts itself), and Zones that don't touch are `terrain-agreement`'s. It doesn't run beside Kept ground. A route that this passes may still be tight; `check_workspace` and the compile's path-width warnings look at that.
- **`route-tests`** (issue #741) runs the Blueprint's declared `tests.routes`, each `{ id, from, to, expect: "arrive" | "blocked", note? }` with `from` and `to` a Zone id or a placed Anchor id. It walks the cells units can step on (the cells `route-open` reads, only through Zones' ground, diagonals only past two open cells) and warns for each test whose result is not the expected one, with the note. Use `arrive` for a way the player must always have and `blocked` for a wall that must hold (a sealed vault, a door the paint closes). Like `route-open` it reads no doodads' or dug pools' paint, so a passing `arrive` still needs an in-game check, while a failing `blocked` is certain. `test_mission` runs the same tests after its compile and reports them. It's not evaluated with no tests declared, and doesn't run beside Kept ground.
- **Entrances** are counted into each held region (the player's, and each enemy base complex), not into each Zone.
- **`readable-approach`** passes a goal that a Beat up to the goal's own lists in `reveals`, or that ground the player can stand on overlooks, and says which. The compiler builds each reveal when its Beat starts: a circle at its point is explored under fog (a Mission starts under Black Mask), and pinged on the minimap once the scenes cued then have handed back. If a later Beat starts during those scenes the ping is dropped, but the ground stays explored. A camera pan, a revealer or a tour on top of that is still the script's job, so check that a scene, cue or Mechanic delivers it. A Beat after the first with no `startsWhen` never starts, so its reveals aren't compiled and the compile warns.
