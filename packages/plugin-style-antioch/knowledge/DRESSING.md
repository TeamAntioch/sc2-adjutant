# Dressing

How to dress a mission for step 4 of [`GUIDE.md`](GUIDE.md): cliff feet first (DL-05), then set pieces with empty ground between them (DL-06), each telling one story with its family of props (DL-08), no solid prop alone. Built rooms are dressed differently, wall first with the floor kept clear: see [`INTERIORS.md`](INTERIORS.md). The blueprint declares the dressing; the compiler places the doodads (`packages/core/src/dressing/expand-dressing.ts`), following this pack's rules in [`rules.ts`](../rules.ts) for what a pile holds, how doodads turn and scale, how lips clump, a broken run's wear and the landmark warnings.

## What the blueprint says

- **Cliff dressing**, map-wide: `dressing.cliffs` has a `natural` pile and a `built` pile, and a `spacing` (one pile every `spacing` cells of cliff on average, default 6). A cliff takes the pile for its cliff set's kind. Each pile is heaped at a cliff foot (DL-05): 1-2 of its `large` pieces against the cliff, 2-4 `small` ones round them at mixed sizes, sometimes a `ground` decal under it (60%) and an `accent` (25%). A pile's `tint` ("r,g,b") colours its members to match each other and the cliff; a member's own `tint` or `scale` range wins.
  - Natural pile: boulders and rocks in the cliff's colour as `large`, rubble, stone chunks and dirt mounds as `small` (tint grey ones warm or cool to match), a crack decal as `ground`. The compiler pushes a natural pile right into the cliff (its large pieces overlap the slope, with the Editor's "ignore placement" set on every piece) at the foot's ground height; a built pile stands just off the wall.
  - Built pile: a wreck (a destroyed tank, a crushed trailer) and a shrunken building piece (`scale` 0.35-0.5) as `large`, debris as `small`, a debris splat as `ground`, smoke as `accent`.
- **Edge lines** (DL-18): `dressing.edges` has a `built` line and a `lip` line for the whole map; a zone's own `edges.built` / `edges.lip` replaces either, or turns it off with `"none"`. A line is `members` plus how it runs:
  - Built: curbs or trim end to end round a `surface: "built"` zone, wherever its edge meets other ground on its level; `step` 1.6 (3-10's curbs), `turn` 90 (most trim lies across its yaw), `inset` 0.3 inside, `variations` to keep to straight pieces. The ground stays level a cell past the edge, so curbs don't sit on bumps. `gaps` breaks a run (a worn floor); `breaks` wears the breaks: `caps` are wreckage shrunk to noise (0.08-0.16) laid over each cut end, so no piece ends square, and `debris` is strewn in the gaps. `Aiur_City_Debris` as caps and `Aiur_Destroyed_Protoss_Units` / `Aiur_City_Curb_Destroyed` as debris suit gold Protoss trim; anything at a recognisable size reads as a crash, not wear.
  - Lip: along every cliff's crest (a mesa's top, a pit's rim), on the high side. By default it grows wild: dense clumps with bare stretches between (`clumps` 0.35, roughly the share left bare), packed `step` 0.5 apart from right on the crest (`inset` -0.95, at the lip's height) to a cell or two in, with how far in each clump reaches wandering along the edge. Plants, small rocks and grass belong here; even rows read as a garden. A planted or built line (a colonnade) sets `clumps: 0`, its own `step`, `inset` and `aligned: true`.
- **Shore dressing**: `dressing.shores` heaps piles, a fringe of plants and sunken pieces along every water pool's waterline; see [`WATER.md`](WATER.md).
- **Set pieces**: placements of kind `set-piece`, each with:
  - `story`: what happened here, in one sentence;
  - `at`: the centre (else the Zone's centre); `radius` in cells (default 6);
  - `anchor`: one or two structures or large props at the centre;
  - `props`: mid-size props in a ring (3 each by default);
  - `clutter`: small scatter and decals, densest near the middle and allowed to heap (6 each by default);
  - `density`: sparse, normal or dense (halves or doubles props and clutter);
  - `nestle: true` stands its members by other doodads, as a terrainer ignores placement in the Editor to tuck props against a big piece: it skips only the spacing check against other doodads (each piece otherwise keeps 0.8 × the larger of the two pieces' spacing by size: tiny 0.6, small 1, medium 2, large 3.5, huge 5 cells, times scale, so a huge vault pushes props 4 cells out). The map's edge, the level and the blocked cells (ramps, crossings, water, cliffs) still apply, and nestled pieces are written with "ignore placement";
  - any member may be `{ "type": …, "count": n, "scale": [min, max], "tint": "r,g,b", "nestle": true|false, "yaw": degrees, "against": "wall" }` instead of a name, or `{ "composite": "<recipe id>", "count": n, "nestle": …, "yaw": …, "against": … }` for copies of a Doodad Composite (below). A member's `nestle` wins over the set piece's. `yaw` turns every copy's front that way instead of a random turn: degrees counterclockwise from east, so 270 is south. A doodad whose front is measured in core's `doodad-facings.json`, or read for sure from its catalog sheet (`doodad-sheet-readings.json`), stands at `yaw` less its front. The compile names every piece turned by a sheet reading in one warning. A composite's whole shape turns by the recipe's `front`, or by the front its members agree on. Anything with no front takes `yaw` as its rotation, and the compile warns. The 45° snap for built pieces doesn't touch it. `against: "wall"` (instead of `yaw`) backs every copy onto the nearest wall in the radius, front to the room, at its measured stand-off and height ([doodad-facings.md](../../../docs/reference/doodad-facings.md)).
- **Doodad Composites** (DL-07): a recipe is a shape no single asset provides (a missile tower, a crowned column, a sunk stain), built from manipulated stock doodads. Recipes live in the blueprint's `composites` and the active Style Pack's shared library, this pack's `composites.json` (nine recipes: four from the Exemplar Maps, `missile-tower`, `conduit-ring`, `crowned-column` and `sunk-tar`; four cut from Blizzard's interiors, `console-bank`, `supply-stack`, `pipe-manifold` and `steam-hatch`, kept in `blizzard-composites.json` and not yet looked at in the Editor; and the author's `crusher-press`, built in the Editor and kept in `author-composites.json`); the mission's own wins on an id clash. Against repetition (the author: "I don't want my maps to just look like we're repeating ourselves"), the shape is exact but each copy turns (45° steps for a `built` recipe), takes a size from the recipe's `scale` range, and picks its own variant for members marked `variation: "any"`. A `landmark` is meant to be rare: the compile warns past 3 copies, or when two copies in different set pieces stand within 20 cells (a deliberate pair inside one set piece is fine). A `detail` repeats freely. Use one only where a shape is missing; places come from mood mixtures, never from a recipe. Capture a shape whole (an arc cut from 3-4's ring machine read as loose worms), and keep a one-piece recipe for things meant to scatter (the tar): a run of trim along a hall's edge is a placement along an edge, not a recipe, and scattered it reads as random. Any model in the game can be a member: a recipe's `actors` (`{ "id", "model", "scale", "animate", "animation" }`) make doodads from unit, weapon or effect models, written to the map's ActorData (3-4's missile tower is the Scout's missile model; still unless `animate`, which keeps the stock Stand loop; `"animation": "Unpowered"` instead plays that Stand variant from creation, looping, so a machine can start powered down).

- **Backdrops** (DL-01): a large backdrop gets no floor clutter, but it isn't left bare. Cover it first (`water`, or `sky` with `hideLowestLevel` or a low, dense fog), then give its Zone a few sparse set pieces of large silhouettes that stand up out of the cover (boulders, ruins, fallen towers) with wide empty ground between, and hang rock or structure down the walls under the rims. Pick the cover from the mood: lava reads hot, fog and water read cold (a cold, ruined world takes fog with stone rubble and broken ruins).

The compiler keeps a set piece on its centre's cliff level, off ramps and cliff cells, and clear of connection crossing points, so it can't block a route. Built doodads snap to 45°, natural ones turn freely (DL-11); scale is 1. Every doodad has its own seed: editing one set piece, or adding a type to it, moves nothing else. Each set piece becomes a named Editor region and a labelled circle in `npm run preview`.

A member that finds no room in 8 tries is dropped, and the compile warns for each member that placed fewer than its count: `Set piece <id>: placed 0 of 3 AvernusBarrier (no room after 8 tries); …`. Read those warnings after each compile: give the member room (a bigger `radius`, fewer pieces) or nestle it.

Each catalog entry records its `package`. Doodads from campaign packages need their campaign as a map dependency, or the Editor shows them as unknown: `npm run compile-map` adds the missing one (Void campaign or Void Story) and says so, or, when the blueprint depends on a custom mod, warns instead. Prefer base-mod doodads (`package` `mods/liberty.sc2mod`) when a campaign one has a near equivalent (`Tarsonis_DeadMarine` over the campaign's `MarineCorpse`).

To make a recipe, the author builds the shape in the Editor from stock doodads (stack, tilt, stretch, sink, tint), draws a region round it and saves; `npm run capture-composite -- <blueprint> <map>.SC2Map "<region name>" [--kind landmark|detail] [--style natural|built] [--scale min,max]` writes the doodads inside it (offsets from their middle, heights above the ground there) into the blueprint's `composites`, with any of the map's own doodads made from models among them as `actors`. Then mark members whose variant isn't part of the shape `"variation": "any"`. Move a recipe into the pack's library only once it proves worth reusing (the author expects that to be rare); `packages/core/scripts/build-composites.mjs` rebuilds the exemplar recipes and appends the Blizzard ones from `blizzard-composites.json`, so add a non-exemplar recipe there, not to `composites.json`.

A recipe can also be composed from a description of the shape, with no Editor capture: write it into the blueprint's `composites` with the fields a captured one has (each member's `x`, `y` and `height` in cells from the centre, `yaw`, `pitch` and `roll` in radians, `scale`, `tint`, `variation`, `noFootprint`, and `actors` for members made from models). Give it a `kind` and a `style` like any recipe, and a `front` (degrees at yaw 0) if it backs onto a wall and its members' measured fronts don't settle it, so each copy still varies and a landmark still warns, and mark every member whose variant isn't part of the shape `"any"`. A composed shape hasn't been seen yet: compile it alone on open ground and look at it in the Editor before a set piece uses it.

## Effects

Effect doodads (steam, sparks, fire, smoke, haze, wind) are accents (DL-19). The catalog marks them `role: effect`.

- **On their cause.** Steam at a hatch or vent, the jet sunk under the floor so only its plume shows (`steam-hatch`); sparks over broken panels or debris; fire on a breach or wreck; smoke at a fault or from a machine; haze tinted to the light. An effect on bare floor reads as a glitch.
- **Few.** About one per room in Blizzard's hero-mission interiors (0.3 per 100 floor cells), 1-1.5 per 100 in a wrecked room. Steam in particular is rare: `SpaceSteamJet` appears 7 times in the whole campaign, all on one map. Industrial atmosphere comes mostly from static exhaust props (`SpacePlatformExhaust`, `ExhaustPipes`), fire, smoke and sparks.
- **In backdrops**, effects add motion (rising heat, wind, drifting dust), as on Blizzard's platform maps: Sky Shield's `Reentry_Heat` below the platform, Phantoms of the Void's `WindVertical`.

## Height, stacking and overlap

Use the vertical (DL-19): stand most wall dressing at the wall's foot, at floor height, pushed into the cliff (Blizzard: 71% of wall pieces); hang only signs, screens, rails and some cables, 1.5-3 up the face; stack crates; sink what should show only its top. A piece's height is read against the room's floor: over a cliff face the ground is mid-slope. Push solid pieces into each other with ignore placement, so a group reads as one mass. Composite members take `height`, set-piece `added` doodads and `edits` take `heightOffset`, and a doodad anchor stands `position.z` above the ground with the `HeightOffset` flag. Blizzard sets a height by hand on over half its interior doodads and ignores placement on 71%.

## Edge taste

What makes an edge read right, and which of it the pack's rules already do. A point no rule covers is for whoever writes the blueprint or dresses in the Editor.

- **Natural edges grow; they aren't planted.** Plants, rocks and rubble along any natural edge (a cliff's lip, a pit's rim, a shore) gather in dense clumps, about three times as dense as an even line would be, with bare stretches between, and sit right at the edge (plants may hang over it). A piece a cell back from the edge looks timid, and an even row reads as a garden. On lips this is the `wild-lips-and-wear` rule's `lipClumps` with the lip line's defaults (`clumps` 0.35, `step` 0.5, `inset` -0.95), and a natural cliff pile is pushed into the rock by `cliff-piles`. A shore's `fringe` isn't clumped: each waterline cell takes a piece on `fringeChance` alone, so keep it dense ([`WATER.md`](WATER.md)). Placing by hand, set the Editor's "ignore placement" to get pieces onto the crest.
- **Ignore placement, but only plants hang over.** "Ignore placement" is fine wherever it gets the look: onto a crest, tucked against a big piece, pushed into a slope. Plants may lean and hang out over a cliff's edge. Rocks and anything else solid mustn't dangle off it, part of the piece out over the drop with nothing under it, unless the story wants it there (a wreck teetering on the brink). The lip line enforces this: a piece whose catalog `role` isn't `foliage` stands at least half its size in from the crest, and is dropped where any of the ground round it is cliff or off the top (a mesa's corner), while foliage keeps the lip line's `inset` -0.95 out over the cliff mesh. Set pieces and composites already stay off cliff cells. Rock hung down a backdrop's walls (DL-01) isn't dangling: it stands in the drop against the wall. No rule checks hand-placed doodads or `edits`, so look along the rims in the Editor (rocks on a chasm's lips can hang out over it).
- **Built edges stay even.** Curbs, trim and anything planted or built (a colonnade) run at an even `step` with `clumps: 0`, which is how core spaces any line without a clump rule. Curbs want level ground under them: core levels the ground a cell past a built edge, and the floor's built texture should reach right under the curbs (`inset` 0.3 keeps them inside the floor).
- **Wear shrinks to noise.** Wreckage marking a broken run is shrunk until it reads as noise, not as the model it is: about 0.1 of its size. The `wild-lips-and-wear` rule's `breakWear` lays two caps over each cut end at 0.08-0.16 and up to three bits of debris in the gap at 0.12-0.3; wear placed by hand takes the same sizes.
- **Big wreckage near trim reads as a wreck.** Protoss wreckage at a quarter of its size or more (destroyed units, building pieces) next to trim reads as a crashed ship, not a worn floor. No rule checks it: keep large wreckage out of a line's `breaks` and away from trim, unless the place is a wreck's set piece. For broken trim without loose wreckage, a destroyed variant of the trim can go into the run itself: 3-9 lays `Protoss_Base_GroundTrim_Destroyed` 140 times beside 49 intact pieces, at scale 0.6.

## Finding doodads

The catalog is two files joined by doodad id. [`packages/core/data/doodad-extracted.json`](../../../packages/core/data/doodad-extracted.json) holds every doodad with its Editor `name`, its picture (`image`, a file in the sc2-mapping-reference gallery; where the gallery lacks one, its local WhiteoutFlakes renders in `packages/core/data/thumbnails/doodads/<id>_00_a0.png` to `_a3.png`, which exist only once `npm run thumbnails` has run). `node_modules/@team-antioch/sc2-asset-catalog/data/doodad-descriptions.json`, published by sc2-asset-catalog, holds, for about 2,400 of them, a description from its picture (the gallery's, or for entries marked `rendered`, a WhiteoutFlakes thumbnail from `npm run thumbnails`; their `size` comes from the model's bounding box): `look`, `kind` (natural, built, creature), `size` (tiny to huge, against a Marine), `role` (cliff, structure, prop, clutter, foliage, decal, effect), `story` tags (battle, wreckage, ruin, abandoned, camp, mining, industry, lab, temple, city, military, zerg-infestation, graveyard, crash…) and `biome`. A `state` (destroyed, abandoned…) means the picture shows the intact model and the state comes from the name. To see a doodad, read its sheet: a 512 px JPEG of its four turntable renders on grey, at `packages/core/data/catalog-sheets/doodads/<id>.jpg` (a terrain object's is named after its model). `search_doodads` gives each result's sheet as an absolute path when it's on this machine. Sheets are rendered game assets, so they're git-ignored and built on the author's machine (`npm run thumbnails`, then `npm run catalog-sheets`; the Host Adapter needs `model.render`). If the file is missing, say so and fall back to the gallery picture, which needs the sc2-mapping-reference repo, or the `description`. Search by words:

```sh
python3 -c "import json,sys; d=json.load(open('node_modules/@team-antioch/sc2-asset-catalog/data/doodad-descriptions.json')); [print(k,'|',v['role'],v['size'],'|',v['look']) for k,v in d.items() if all(w in (k+json.dumps(v)).lower() for w in sys.argv[1:])]" battle desert
```

The Campaign Mod's doodads are described the same way in the Workspace's `mods/<id>/doodad-catalog.json`, with sheets under that mod's folder. `asset_catalog` lists them with their descriptions and the absolute paths of their sheets, and `index-mod-doodads` renders new ones for an agent to describe ([mod-doodad-index.md](../../../docs/reference/mod-doodad-index.md)).

**One-sided models.** Some rock models have no back faces, so from behind they show as a hole or a black block. Keep them away from void edges, from anywhere the gameplay camera sees their far side, and from cutscene sets where the camera can swing round them. Known so far:
- `Shakuras_Tall_Rock_Chunk`: a hole from behind on a chasm rim, and a black block when it stands in the open where the camera sees round it.
- `Shakuras_Rock_Chunk_Small`: the same family; treat it as one-sided until it has been checked.

## Steps

1. **Cliffs first** (DL-05): choose `dressing.cliffs` for maps with cliffs; skip it on flat or built maps. Then edges (DL-18): kerb built zones, and grow the lips of cliffs the player sees from above.
2. **Set pieces** (DL-06): a few per mission, where the story put something (a crash, a fight, a camp), with open ground between them. In a built room, follow [`INTERIORS.md`](INTERIORS.md)'s order instead: walls, corners, centrepiece, edges, floor, lights and effects, each place a kit. For each, write its `story`, then pick its family from the catalog by story tag and biome (DL-08): one or two anchors, three or four props, four or more clutter types at mixed sizes. Err dense and varied (DL-06), and give every solid prop company within 2.5 cells (DL-08). A fight leaves bodies and gore (corpses, blood decals), not only wreckage. Check each member's colour against the ground texture: a pale prop on matching sand vanishes, so pick another or ring it with decals. Present them as a table (set piece, story, members).
3. **Write and compile.** Done when `npm run compile-map` succeeds; the author looks in the Editor.

## Feedback

Name the set piece the comment is about (it's a region in the Editor and a label in the preview), then sort the comment:
- **About the set piece** ("more debris", "swap the barrels", "move it north", "make it bigger"): edit its entry and recompile. Nothing else moves.
- **About one doodad** ("that rock's in the wrong place"): the author fixes it in the Editor and saves, then `npm run sync -- <blueprint> <map>.SC2Map` reads it back. A moved, turned, tilted, rescaled, retinted, swapped, "ignore placement"-toggled or deleted set-piece or cliff-pile doodad becomes an entry in its set piece's `edits` (or `dressing.cliffs.edits`, `dressing.shores.edits`, `dressing.edges.edits`), keyed by its seed, so the set piece stays procedural; a doodad added inside a set piece's circle joins its `added`, and one added elsewhere becomes a doodad anchor. Sync from the map compiled from this blueprint, before changing the blueprint again: anything the blueprint makes that the map lacks counts as deleted. When the author would rather describe the fix, freeze the set piece with `npm run freeze-set-piece -- <blueprint> <set piece id>` (ordinary anchors `<id>-1`, `-2`… in the same places; the set piece loses its region) and edit the anchor. The same sync reads back units, points and cameras (into their anchors) and regions (a zone's redrawn region becomes its trigger area; a region the author draws joins `regions`).
- **After a sync,** read its warnings at the next compile: an edit whose doodad no longer exists (its set piece's params or its cliff changed) is reported, not applied.
- **About taste in general** ("battle sites need bodies", "never barrels on ash"): record it in the pack's `LESSONS.md` with the date and the author's words, as a new lesson or a refinement, and follow it from then on.
