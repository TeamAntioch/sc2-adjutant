You'll build a **Style Pack** from your own maps: your visual taste, written down so that every session dresses your Missions the way you would. Its evidence is a handful of **Exemplar Maps**: maps you made, edited or admire. Claude studies each one, captures its dressed places in game, and measures what your maps agree on against Blizzard's campaign maps. What your maps share and Blizzard's mostly don't is your taste. Claude drafts it as candidate **Design Lessons** and moods, and brings them to you one at a time. Only what you keep goes into the pack. Then the [`exemplar-design`](/skills/exemplar-design/) skill reads the pack as it reads any Style Pack.

The [`style-pack-builder`](/skills/style-pack-builder/) skill runs it all. Here it works in the Workspace from [Your first Mission](/tutorials/first-mission/), with four maps: *Harbour* and *Foundry*, which the mapper made, *Outpost*, which they edited, and *Relic*, someone else's map they admire. Use your own: three or more give the clearest result. The in-game captures need a Host Adapter that can run camera tours. Without one, the pack is built from the maps' data alone.

The turns below are written by hand. Claude's words will differ in your session, and so will the numbers, since they come from your maps. The tool calls are the ones it makes.

A Style Pack is a Plugin, and this one is a folder in your Workspace. [`scaffold_style_pack`](/tools/scaffold_style_pack/) writes its manifest, `sc2-adjutant-plugin.json`, and these:

- `exemplar-maps/exemplars.json`, the list of Exemplar Maps, empty for now;
- `exemplar-maps/candidate-lessons.md`, for drafts, and `exemplar-maps/LESSONS.md`, for the Design Lessons you ratify, each with the format its entries follow;
- `knowledge/GUIDE.md`, a stub, written last;
- `exemplar-maps/.gitignore`, which keeps the in-game captures and frames out of git.

It then sets the Workspace config's `stylePack` to `./style-pack`. If the Workspace already names a Style Pack, the tool refuses and names it. Claude asks whether the new pack is to replace it, and makes it only on a yes. Give the pack another folder or name if you like: its name is a Plugin name, lowercase, `style-<campaign id>` by default.

A **Map Study** is a map's look as measurements: its texturing, cliffs, doodad dressing, lighting and composition. [`study_exemplar`](/tools/study_exemplar/) writes it as `study.json` in `exemplar-maps/<id>/`, beside its layer images: the map from above as textures, elevation, doodads and more. They're what Claude looks at for a map with no frames.

A **place** is a cluster of doodads: doodads within 2.5 cells of each other, with a wide cluster split into 16-cell tiles, and anything under 8 visible doodads dropped. [`find_places`](/tools/find_places/) records each place's centre, size and what it's made of in `exemplar-maps/mixtures.json`: its **motifs**, such as machinery, cargo, rock or vegetation, as shares of its doodads, and its most-used types. The places are where the in-game captures look, and what moods are made of. Claude always runs it over every map, since it rewrites the file for the maps it's given alone.

</Objective>

Without a Host Adapter that has `editor.captureTour`, [`capture_exemplar`](/tools/capture_exemplar/) says what the machine lacks and captures nothing. Claude goes on from the studies and layer images, and the candidates cite numbers and layer images instead of frames.

<Objective id="report" title="Read the consistency report">

The **consistency report** measures your maps on lesson-neutral **Measures**, numbers that carry no Design Lesson, such as the share of rocks turned to a 45° step, or point lights per 100 cells. For each Measure, it finds what your maps agree on and sets it against Blizzard's campaign maps.

<Claude>
<ToolCall name="exemplar_report" args={{}}>measures the four maps' studies against Blizzard's campaign maps</ToolCall>

The report, trimmed:

```text
Exemplar Maps: harbour (made), foundry (made), outpost (edited), relic (approved).
Blizzard's spread is over 81 campaign maps; the baseline couldn't measure nova01, nova02, …, nova09, so its spreads leave them out.

Distinctive: the maps agree, outside Blizzard's spread (p10 to p90):
- lighting.pointLightsPerHundredCells (point lights (LightOmni) per 100 playable, non-ramp cells): harbour, foundry, outpost at 0.39 to 0.44, above Blizzard's spread; Blizzard p10 0, median 0.005, p90 0.176 over 81 maps, the maps' median above 96% of them; outside it: relic 0.02
- yawSnapping.natural (share of visible natural props (rocks, trees, foliage) turned to within a degree of a 45-degree step): harbour, foundry, outpost at 0.63 to 0.74, above Blizzard's spread; …; outside it: relic 0.05

Common practice: the maps agree, as Blizzard's maps do:
- doodadDensity.perHundredCells (…): harbour, foundry, outpost, relic at 4.4 to 6.1; Blizzard p10 2.234, median 4.495, p90 9.011 over 81 maps, …
- …

Questions for the mapper: the maps don't agree:
- blend.width (…): from 0.74 to 0.8, the largest group harbour, outpost; …; outside the largest group: foundry 1.52, relic 1.66
```

Blizzard's spreads leave out Nova Covert Ops' missions: the install the Blizzard Baseline was built from held none of their data.

Two habits are yours: your maps light their places with many more point lights than Blizzard's do, and turn their rocks and trees to 45° steps, where Blizzard's mostly don't. Relic doesn't share either, which fits: it's someone else's. How densely you dress is common practice: every one of your maps sits inside Blizzard's spread. Your maps disagree on how wide their textures blend: Harbour and Outpost blend in thin seams, Foundry and Relic in wide soft bands.
</Claude>

The report sorts each Measure into one of these, in this order:

- **Distinctive:** your maps agree, and wholly outside Blizzard's spread, from its 10th to its 90th percentile. This is your own taste, where candidate lessons come from.
- **Common practice:** your maps agree, as Blizzard's maps do. Every SC2 campaign map does it, so it's rarely worth a lesson.
- **Questions:** your maps don't agree. Claude asks you which way is yours.
- Then what it can't place: Measures your maps agree on that no Blizzard map has a value for, and Measures too few of your maps have a value for.

Your maps **agree** on a Measure when a group of them, close together, holds at least two maps and two thirds of the maps' weight: a `made` map weighs 3, an `edited` one 2 and an `approved` one 1. Close together means no further apart than the middle half of Blizzard's maps, from its 25th to its 75th percentile. The maps outside the group are its outliers. Within each kind, the heaviest evidence comes first.

The comparison is always with Blizzard's campaign maps: every mission of Wings of Liberty, Heart of the Swarm and Legacy of the Void, 81 maps. sc2-adjutant ships their Measures as the **Blizzard Baseline**, so you don't need the game's files for it. [`exemplar_report`](/tools/exemplar_report/) reads only the studies, so it runs without your source maps.

</Objective>

A candidate gives the rule it would become, its numbers, the maps that support it (and those that don't) and two to four frames. Each frame is named by its map, the lighting it was captured under and the place's centre in cells, or its stop in a grid tour, and described by what it shows. It also names its **Blueprint hook**: the part of a Mission Blueprint it would shape.

A **mood** is a kind of place in your maps. Its rule gives the motifs a place must have and their least share. A place takes the first mood whose rule it passes, and the last mood, with no rule, takes the rest.

</Objective>

The frames a kept lesson cites are copied to `exemplar-maps/<id>/evidence/`, which git ignores, for your later reference. A Blueprint hook can name the slot a Style Rule could later fill, such as `yaw`; a Style Pack built this way has no Style Rules of its own yet, so its lessons guide the design rather than the compile.
