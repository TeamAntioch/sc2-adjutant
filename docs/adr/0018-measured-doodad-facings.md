# Measured Doodad Facings

A doodad's model faces whichever way its artist built it, and the game records no front. Generated dressing used to assume a model's front points along its rotation, and an Interior Dressing kit's `yaw` "facing the room" was written on that assumption. Most of a Kept city map's factory wall pieces came out a quarter-turn wrong, and they stood half on the wall's cliff slope. So core now keeps each doodad's **front** and **placement** as measured by eye in the Editor, in `packages/core/data/doodad-facings.json` ([doodad-facings.md](../reference/doodad-facings.md)), and the dressing reads it.

- **Front** is the direction the doodad's face points when it stands at rotation 0, in degrees counterclockwise from east. **Placement** is where it belongs: `wall-foot`, `wall-face`, `wall-top` or `free`. Each placement has a default stand-off from the wall zone's polygon edge and a default height, and a doodad may give its own.
- **Only what the author has checked by eye goes in.** A doodad that isn't in the file is unknown. Nothing derives a default for it, whether from its kind, its name or its family.
- **A set piece member's `yaw` now means where its front faces**, not its rotation. A measured doodad stands at `yaw` − front, a composite turns by its recipe's `front` or the front its measured members agree on, and anything unmeasured keeps the old meaning and a warning. When this changed, the only `yaw` in the author's Missions was on a recipe with no measured members (a `lit-sanctum` recipe), so none of their compiled output moved.
- **`against: "wall"`** (#530's wall-backed members) backs a member onto the nearest wall in its set piece, front to the room, at its measured stand-off and height.
- **The facing strip** (`npm run facing-strip`) is how the file grows. It compiles a test map with one of each doodad at rotation 0 against south-facing walls. The author adjusts each one in the Editor, and the read-back turns what they saved into entries.

## Considered Options

- **Derive fronts from Kept maps.** Rejected as the source of record. A model's most common turn on our hand-built maps is a hint, not a fact: `ZergWallDecorTwoHigh` faces + 270° in 82% of its placements, and `DOM_GroundTrims` + 180° in only 58%. A hint could prioritise what to measure.
- **A front field on the doodad catalog's `description`.** Rejected. `doodad-catalog.json` is regenerated by `build-doodad-catalog.mjs` and describes a doodad's picture. A facing is measured on a map, and it carries placement data the catalog has no words for. A file of its own keeps the two sources apart, as `model-footprints.json` and `model-guide.json` do.
- **Key by model file instead of doodad type.** Rejected for now. The dressing, the Blueprint and the Editor all speak in doodad types. A doodad's variations can be different models, so the file says it was measured on one, and a variation found to differ can be recorded later.
- **Keep `yaw` as the rotation and add a new field for facing.** Rejected. The pack's guides already told agents to write `yaw` as "facing the room", and two fields for one turn would invite exactly the mistake this fixes.

## Consequences

- `getDoodadFacing`, `rotationToFace` and `compositeFront` (`packages/core/src/catalog/doodad-facings.ts`) are the readers. `search_doodads` returns a game doodad's facing.
- A Campaign Mod doodad, or a game doodad whose model the mod replaced, has no facing of its own yet. The game doodad's entry applies by type.
- A wall-top piece can't be placed against a wall until its stand-off is measured.
- Hand-placed doodad anchors and `added` doodads keep the rotation they're given. Whoever places them turns them with the table.
