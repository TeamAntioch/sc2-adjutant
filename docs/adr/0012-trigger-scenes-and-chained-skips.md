# Trigger Scenes From Shot Lists, and One Skip for a Chain

A Screenplay scene that teaches the map (the start, the goal, where the threat lies) is a **Trigger Scene**: Cinematic Mode on the Mission's own map, the Blueprint's camera Anchors and the scene's lines as Transmissions. Like a Cinematic ([ADR 0009](./0009-cinematics-from-shot-lists-and-sets-as-data.md)), it is compiled from a shot list in the Workspace rather than built by hand in the Editor's triggers as Kept Content, which would be rebuilt every time the scene or the map changed.

**Where it lives.** `missions/<id>/trigger-scenes/<id>.json`, listed in the Blueprint's `screenplay.script.triggerScenes: [{ id, scene }]` (or `[{ id, outcome: "victory" }]` for a victory outro with no scene), beside `cinematics/`. Not `scenes/`: a Screenplay already has scenes, numbered, and the folder would read as theirs. A scene plays as one Cinematic or one Trigger Scene, never both. Shots name camera Anchors by id and lines by sound ID, so moving an Anchor in the Editor (and syncing it back) or recording a voice moves the scene with it.

**How it plays.** Blizzard's four triggers, run in turn with wait: Setup, Cinematic, Cinematic End and Cleanup (the cinematography skill's RECIPES.md "Trigger scene"). Only the Cinematic trigger holds the skippable wait, so a skip ends that trigger and nothing else, and the end and the cleanup run the same either way (T1, T2). The template's timings (a 1.5 s fade in; a fade out over 2.0 s, or 0.5 s after a skip; a handback over 1.5 s, or 1.0 s after a skip) are fixed by the compiler, not declared per scene, so T3 and H2 hold by construction. A move's length is taken from the voice files at compile time, as a Cinematic's timeline is; the lines play waited, so the engine keeps them in order whatever the voice files turn out to be.

**Chaining.** Scenes cued back to back on one cue share one skip and one cleanup (T1, T2; H3 applies to the last of them). Today that is a Cinematic followed straight away by a Trigger Scene, as a mission's opening Cinematic may be followed by its Scene 2. The chain is known at compile time from the cues, and only a Cinematic some cue pairs with a Trigger Scene gets the chain's code, so any other Cinematic compiles exactly as before:

- A Cinematic that hands on records whether its run was watched to the end (`cinematic_<id>_completed`; a skip ends its watch before it's set).
- Its cleanup leaves the screen black (a `CinematicFade` out under the dialog), Cinematic Mode on and the Music soundtrack paused. The Trigger Scene's handback is the one that turns the mode off and brings the music back.
- The cue then starts the Trigger Scene, copying the Cinematic's skip into the scene's own `trigger_scene_<id>_skipped` with no wait between, so no other cue's run can change it. A Trigger Scene started from any other cue gets `false`. Finding it set, Setup goes straight to Cleanup, so the world lands in its one final state.
- Whether a Cinematic and the Trigger Scene after it are paired is one test, made where the cue's actions are built: the Cinematic can hand on (some cue pairs them) and the next scene on this cue is a Trigger Scene.

The Cleanup waits for its fade in, so what the cue plays next (the goal timers at mission start, a later scene) starts once control is back.

AI time is paused by each scene and put back by each cleanup. Between them it runs for no game time, so neither records the other's pause as the mission's.

## Consequences

- A chain of Trigger Scenes (after a Cinematic or not) shares one skip the same way (#427): each but the last hands on (`trigger_scene_<id>_hand_on`), its cleanup leaving the screen black and Cinematic Mode on, and the next copies its `completed` into its own `skipped`, and whether the Music is paused into its `after_cinematic`. Only the last of a chain hands back, so a scene that only ever hands on may not declare a `handback`; one cued both ways uses it where it plays last, but may not declare its pings, objective or `linesFrom`, which a cue it hands on from would drop. Handing on, a `fade: false` scene still fades out, so the next scene starts from black. The first scene of the chain saves the player's camera, and the ones after keep that save (`camera_saved`). A Trigger Scene before a Cinematic still plays each with its own skip until the chain grows to cover it. A scene that hands on hasn't handed back, so a `scenePlayed` cue on it (#461) fires once the chain's last scene has, with every other scene's of the chain, never under the black between them.
- Clocks are held at the cue, not in the scenes: a run of Cinematics and Trigger Scenes back to back runs "Clocks Hold" before its first and "Clocks Release" after its last (H4), pausing the goal timers and each timer a Mechanic declares in `clocks`, and unpausing only the ones it paused.
- The handback camera at mission start is the Trigger Scene's `handback.camera`, else the camera saved in Setup (`CameraSave`/`CameraRestore`), which at mission start is the map's start camera.
- A victory outro (`outro: "victory"`, #428) is the one Trigger Scene that never hands back: it plays last on a victory outcome's cue, its cleanup leaves the screen black with Cinematic Mode on, and the victory trigger's `GameOver` follows it on both paths. What it would put back for play, it leaves, since play doesn't resume. One with no Screenplay scene (#463) is listed by `outcome: "victory"` in place of a `scene`: it has no lines and no cue, and plays last on every victory condition, as the last entry of that condition's run, so the scene before it hands on to it as on a cue. The first victory condition met plays it; the others then start nothing.
- A Trigger Scene starts the moment its cue fires, never waiting for an in-play line: it cuts off only the in-play line playing, replays it after the handback, and its Cleanup clears only its own line, not every transmission ([ADR 0013](./0013-trigger-scenes-pre-empt-in-play-lines.md)). Across a chain, each scene takes the lines from the one before.
- A scene may open with no fade (`open.fade: false`, #484): no black at Setup, and the first shot's camera reached by an eased pan from the player's over `easeMs` (1 s when left out), a template timing the scene gives itself, in place of the cut and the fade in. A scene a chain takes over in has no player's camera to ease from, only the black the scene before hands on under, so it can't open this way; the chain's first scene can. Setup's changes under black (the staged cast, marks, hidden live units, Light, fog and mask) would happen in sight, so the checks refuse each beside it.
- Everything a scene changes it must also put back in its cleanup, on both paths. Later additions (staged cast, live units moved, Light and fog, the black mask) each extend the one Cleanup trigger, and an action at a moment in a shot leaves its final state applied there when a skip comes first.
